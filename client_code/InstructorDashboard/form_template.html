Discussion questions

  1. Which turns were hard to label? Could one turn serve more than one purpose, or fit none of the available categories?
  2. What did you use from the surrounding conversation that a classifier looking at one turn might miss?
  3. How might fixed intent categories encourage people to change the way they speak? Who might find that difficult, and what would make the interaction more accessible?
  4. What is lost when a conversation becomes rows labelled Human or Worker, and then get further distilled into a predictive model?
  5. After the class test, examine a misclassification. Does it suggest gaps in training data, an unclear category, or a limitation of the approach?

Notice whether short sentences with few "candy words" (like confirmations or corrections) are harder to classify than ones that mention specific items. This model only looks at which words appear, not their order or context — what does that suggest about its limits, and what would you need to give it to fix those specific mistakes?

How does this actually work?

This uses a simplified "bag-of-words" Naive Bayes classifier: it counts how often each word appears with each intent label in the training data, then for a new sentence, multiplies together how likely each of its words is under each label (with a small correction called Laplace smoothing so unseen words don't break the math), and picks the label with the highest resulting probability. It has no idea about word order, grammar, or meaning beyond word counts — which is exactly why certain sentences trip it up.
